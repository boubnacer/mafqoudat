# Mafqoudat — Product Analysis & Viral Short-Form Video Playbook

Five ready-to-run generative video prompts (Google Flow / Veo, Sora, Kling, Runway) plus the
strategic reasoning behind them. Everything below is grounded in what the product actually
does today — no feature is promised in a video that the platform cannot deliver.

---

## Part 1 — What Mafqoudat actually is (and what that means for marketing)

### The product in one line

Mafqoudat is a community lost-and-found network for the Arab world — people post what they
lost or what they found, the platform quietly matches the two sides, and it tells both people
the moment a credible match appears.

### The parts that matter for content

| Reality of the product | What it means for a video |
|---|---|
| Two-sided: "I lost" and "I found" | Every story has two protagonists. Best short-form format is a **split narrative** that collides at the end. |
| Matching engine scores pairs (category, city, location text, date, keywords) and pushes an alert | This is the only genuinely *filmable* piece of magic. Everything else is a form. **Lead with the alert.** |
| Same category + same city is always a strong match | The promise is simple enough to say in four words: *same thing, same city*. |
| Push notifications reach people who aren't in the app | The payoff shot is a **phone buzzing on a table** — universally readable, no UI literacy needed. |
| Contact happens off-platform (phone / email / WhatsApp) | Do not show in-app chat. Show a **phone call** or a WhatsApp-style ring. Anything else is a lie the product will be judged against. |
| Categories include papers/ID, keys, phones, wallets, pets, jewelry, glasses, bags, money, and missing persons | The high-emotion, high-share categories are **national ID papers, pets, and heirloom jewelry**. Phones are the highest-volume but lowest-emotion. |
| Countries are a first-class dimension; Morocco is the beachhead, en/fr/ar with full RTL | Shoot the world people live in — medina, tram, grand taxi, café terrace, souk — not a generic Western city. |
| Posts expire in 30 days | Creates real urgency: *"the first 48 hours matter"* is honest, not invented. |
| Free, no payments, no ads | The tone can be civic and warm. This is not a growth-hack product; it is closer to a public good. |

### The emotional territory to own

Losing something is never really about the object. It is about the **panic**, the retracing
of steps, the pocket-patting, the sinking feeling in a taxi that has already driven away. And
finding something belonging to a stranger creates its own small burden — *now what do I do
with this?* Mafqoudat sits exactly between those two feelings and resolves both at once.

That is the whole creative strategy: **short-form video should sell the relief, not the app.**
Almost no one searches for a lost-and-found platform before they need one. They discover it,
remember the feeling, and come back on the worst day of their month. So the job of a viral
video is memory, not conversion.

### Why this category can go viral

1. **Universal premise.** Everyone on earth has lost something. Zero explanation needed.
2. **Built-in payoff structure.** Loss → search → reunion is the oldest satisfying story shape
   there is, and it fits in twenty seconds.
3. **Reunion content already performs.** Lost-pet reunions, returned-wallet stories and
   "found this, is it yours?" posts are a proven organic genre across every platform.
4. **It is inherently shareable in-market.** The share is a favour: *"if you ever lose your
   CIN, this exists."* People forward utility to family group chats far more readily than ads.
5. **User-generated content is free and endless.** Every real reunion is a video the community
   makes for you.

### The five content pillars (the prompts below map to these)

1. **Reunion** — the emotional payoff. Highest share rate.
2. **The mechanic** — how two strangers get connected. Highest conversion.
3. **Relatable panic** — comedy about the moment of loss. Highest reach and rewatch.
4. **High-stakes utility** — losing papers/ID, the bureaucratic nightmare. Highest saves.
5. **Community proof** — real people, real streets, documentary texture. Highest trust.

### Distribution notes

- **Format:** 9:16, 1080×1920, 15–30 seconds. Anything longer needs a very strong hook.
- **Hook window:** the first 1.5 seconds decide everything. Open on tension or an object in
  motion — never on a logo, never on a talking founder.
- **Sound-off first.** Most feeds autoplay muted. Every prompt below carries burned-in text
  beats so the story survives with no audio at all.
- **Language:** produce three cuts of each — Darija/Arabic, French, English. Same picture,
  different text and voice-over. The Arabic cut is the primary one for Morocco, Algeria and
  Tunisia; the French cut travels across the Maghreb and the diaspora; the English cut is for
  the Gulf and for app-store/press use.
- **RTL matters even in video.** In the Arabic cuts, text overlays should be right-aligned and
  any on-screen UI mock should be mirrored. Getting this wrong is the fastest way to look
  foreign to the exact audience you want.
- **Never fabricate numbers.** No "50,000 items reunited" unless it is true. The platform's own
  design principle — do not decorate real numbers with invented ones — applies to marketing too.

### Production caveats for AI-generated video (read before generating)

- **Text rendering is unreliable.** Veo and its peers still garble on-screen words, especially
  Arabic script. Generate the footage clean, then add every caption, UI element and logo in
  CapCut / Premiere / After Effects afterwards. All five prompts are written to avoid asking
  the model for legible text.
- **Arabic dialogue is unreliable.** Model lip-sync and pronunciation in Darija are weak. The
  safest workflow: generate with the English or minimal-dialogue version, then dub with a real
  voice, or keep dialogue to one or two short lines and cover the rest with voice-over.
- **Generate in 8-second beats.** Veo generates short clips; Flow's scene-chaining is designed
  for this. Each prompt below is already broken into 8-second shots you can queue as separate
  scenes and stitch on the timeline.
- **Consistency between shots:** in Flow, reuse the same character reference / ingredients
  across scenes, and repeat the full wardrobe and physical description verbatim in every shot
  prompt. Copy-paste the description; do not paraphrase it between shots.
- **Faces and real brands:** avoid naming real people, real companies, or showing real logos.
  Use "a mid-size Moroccan city" rather than a specific named business.

### Prompt 1 — "The Grand Taxi" (Pillar: Reunion)

**The idea.** A woman leaves her handbag in a shared taxi. A driver finds it. Neither knows the
other. The platform closes the loop. The whole video is carried by two faces and one buzzing
phone.

**Why it can travel.** It is the single most common lost-item story in Morocco, told without a
word of product explanation. The viewer is emotionally paid off before they are ever sold to.

**Hook (0.0–1.5s):** a handbag sliding across a car seat as the taxi pulls away — motion,
immediate dread, no context needed.

**Target length:** 24 seconds (three 8-second shots).

#### Style block (repeat verbatim in every shot)

```
Cinematic live-action, shot on 35mm anamorphic, shallow depth of field, warm North African
afternoon light, dust in the air, slightly desaturated earth-tone palette with deep blues.
Handheld with subtle natural movement. Realistic, documentary-adjacent, no stylization, no
text on screen. Vertical 9:16 framing, 1080x1920.
```

#### Shot 1 (0–8s)

```
A beige Mercedes shared taxi pulls away from a curb on a busy street in a mid-size Moroccan
city, late afternoon golden light. Camera is low and inside the back seat, looking down: a
woman's dark red leather handbag slides slowly across the worn vinyl seat as the car turns,
coming to rest against the door. The seat is otherwise empty. Through the rear window, out of
focus, a woman in a mustard-yellow cardigan and a light headscarf walks away from the car
carrying grocery bags, unaware. Dust motes float in the light. The camera pushes gently in on
the abandoned bag.

Audio: engine hum, street traffic, distant car horn, a faint radio playing oud music inside
the taxi. No dialogue.
```

#### Shot 2 (8–16s)

```
Same city, twenty minutes later. Interior of the parked taxi at a quiet corner. A Moroccan
taxi driver in his fifties, grey stubble, olive-green jacket, weathered hands, turns in his
seat and notices the dark red handbag on the back seat. He picks it up carefully, holds it a
moment, looks out at the empty street, and exhales — the expression of a man who now has a
problem he did not ask for. He takes out a scratched smartphone and raises it to photograph
the bag on the seat. Warm light through the windscreen, dust on the dashboard.

Audio: the click of a phone camera shutter, distant street noise, the driver's quiet sigh.
No dialogue.
```

#### Shot 3 (16–24s)

```
A small tiled kitchen, evening, warm lamp light. The woman in the mustard-yellow cardigan sits
at the table, head in her hands, a cup of mint tea untouched in front of her, clearly upset.
Her phone lies face down on the table. The phone buzzes and lights up, vibrating against the
wood. She lifts her head, picks it up, and reads. Her whole face changes — disbelief, then a
hand over her mouth, then a wide relieved smile. She stands up fast, chair scraping, already
raising the phone to her ear.

Audio: a single phone vibration on wood, the scrape of a chair, one sharp intake of breath.
Then quiet. No dialogue.
```

#### Post-production (do this outside the model)

- Burned-in captions, timed to the beats:
  - 0.5s — `She left her bag in the taxi.` / `نساتْ الساك ف الطاكسي.` / `Elle a oublié son sac dans le taxi.`
  - 9s — `He had no idea whose it was.` / `ماكانش عارف ديال شكون.` / `Il ne savait pas à qui il appartenait.`
  - 17s — `They both posted it. That's all it took.` / `بجوج نشْروها. صافي.` / `Ils ont tous les deux publié. C'est tout.`
  - 22s — `Mafqoudat — mafqoudat.com`
- Over shot 3, overlay a small, clean notification card sliding down from the top of the frame
  (build it in your editor, mirrored for the Arabic cut): a match alert naming a bag found in
  the same city. This is the one moment the product appears.
- Music: sparse solo oud or piano, single sustained note under shot 3.

**Caption (Arabic):** نساتْ الساك ديالها ف الطاكسي… وهو لقاها وماعرفش ديال شكون. المنصة هي اللي جمعاتهم. 💛 مفقودات — منصة مجانية للمفقودات ف المغرب.
**Caption (French):** Elle a oublié son sac dans le taxi. Il l'a trouvé sans savoir à qui il était. Gratuit, en 2 minutes. #objetsperdus #Maroc
**Hashtags:** #مفقودات #ضاع_ليا #المغرب #objetsperdus #objetstrouvés #Morocco #lostandfound

### Prompt 2 — "Two Phones" (Pillar: The mechanic)

**The idea.** Perfect split-screen. Left: a man realises his keys are gone. Right: a woman
picks up a set of keys off a café step. Both open the same app. Both phones buzz at the same
instant. The split-screen line dissolves and the two shots become one.

**Why it can travel.** It explains the entire product without a single line of narration, and
the visual gimmick — a split screen that collapses — is the kind of formal trick that earns
rewatches and stitches.

**Hook (0.0–1.5s):** two frames, two strangers, one identical panicked gesture: hands hitting
pockets on the left, hand reaching down on the right.

**Target length:** 22 seconds (three shots).

#### Style block (repeat verbatim in every shot)

```
Cinematic live-action, clean modern commercial look, crisp natural daylight, shallow depth of
field, cool neutral color grade with a single deep blue accent. Locked-off tripod framing with
very slight drift. Symmetrical split-screen composition, hard vertical line down the centre of
a 9:16 vertical frame. Photorealistic, no text on screen, 1080x1920.
```

#### Shot 1 (0–8s)

```
Vertical split-screen, one story on each side of a hard centre line.
LEFT HALF: a young Moroccan man in a grey hoodie stands in front of a blue apartment door in a
narrow sunlit stairwell, patting his pockets frantically, then dropping his backpack to search
it. Frustration building.
RIGHT HALF: a woman in a denim jacket and white headscarf, seated on the step of a café
terrace with a mint tea, notices a set of keys with a small leather fob lying on the tiles
beside the chair leg. She picks them up and turns them over in her hand, looking around for an
owner.
The two halves are lit alike, same time of day, same city.

Audio: on the left, the rustle of fabric and a zip; on the right, the clink of a glass and
distant café chatter. No dialogue.
```

#### Shot 2 (8–15s)

```
Same vertical split-screen composition, same two people, same wardrobe.
LEFT HALF: extreme close-up of the man's hands holding a smartphone, thumb typing quickly, the
screen glow lighting his face from below. His expression is tense and focused.
RIGHT HALF: extreme close-up of the woman's hands, one holding the found keys up in front of a
phone camera to photograph them against the café table, the other steadying the phone.
Both actions are perfectly mirrored in timing and framing — two people doing the same thing
without knowing it.

Audio: soft interface taps, a camera shutter, ambient street sound on both sides. A low
electronic tone begins to rise underneath. No dialogue.
```

#### Shot 3 (15–22s)

```
Same split-screen. Both phones, held in both hands on both sides of the frame, light up and
vibrate at exactly the same moment. Both faces lift from their screens at the same instant,
eyes widening. Then the hard centre line of the split-screen dissolves away and the two shots
merge into a single continuous image: the same street, the man walking in from the left side
of frame and the woman standing up from the café step on the right, meeting near the middle.
He raises a hand in greeting. She holds up the keys. Warm afternoon light, shallow focus,
strangers about to become a small story.

Audio: two phone vibrations landing in perfect unison, then a single warm musical resolve.
Ambient street. No dialogue.
```

#### Post-production

- Overlay the split labels early — left: `LOST` in the status red, right: `FOUND` in the status
  green (mirror sides for the Arabic cut so the reading order works right-to-left).
- On the merge, overlay one line: `Same item. Same city. We tell you both.` /
  `نفس الحاجة. نفس المدينة. كنعلموكم بجوج.` / `Même objet. Même ville. On vous prévient tous les deux.`
- The buzz moment is the money frame — hold it an extra 6 frames longer than feels natural.
- Music: minimal electronic pulse, building tension, one warm chord on the merge.

**Caption (English):** He lost them. She found them. Neither of them knew the other existed. Mafqoudat matches lost and found posts in the same city and tells both people. Free.
**Hashtags:** #lostandfound #مفقودات #objetsperdus #Casablanca #Morocco

### Prompt 3 — "Things I Have Lost This Year" (Pillar: Relatable panic)

**The idea.** A fast, funny, rhythmic montage of objects escaping their owners in impossible
ways — keys sliding into a drain, a phone diving off a café table, sunglasses swallowed by a
sofa — each one on a hard beat. It ends on the one object that is not funny at all: a national
ID card. The tone snaps, and the product lands.

**Why it can travel.** Pure rewatch bait. Escalating, rhythmic, comedic, and the turn at the
end gives it a point. This is the highest-reach concept of the five and the cheapest to remix
into endless variations.

**Hook (0.0–1.0s):** a set of keys already falling in slow motion toward a street drain.

**Target length:** 18 seconds (three shots).

#### Style block (repeat verbatim in every shot)

```
High-speed cinematic macro photography, 120fps slow motion intercut with sharp real-time
snaps, glossy commercial lighting, high contrast, saturated color, dramatic hero-object framing
as if shooting a luxury advertisement — but the subjects are ordinary everyday objects. Punchy,
kinetic, rhythmic. Vertical 9:16, 1080x1920, no text on screen.
```

#### Shot 1 (0–7s) — three losses, rapid cuts

```
Three rapid-fire vignettes, roughly two seconds each, all shot as dramatic slow-motion
hero-object shots with glossy advertising lighting.
1. A set of house keys tumbles end over end in extreme slow motion and drops cleanly through
   the slot of a street drain on a wet pavement. Water glints.
2. A smartphone slides off the edge of a marble café table in slow motion and vanishes into the
   dark gap between two cushions of a rattan chair.
3. A pair of sunglasses, resting on a car roof, slides off as the car begins to move, spinning
   once in the air against a bright sky.
Each vignette ends the instant the object disappears from frame.

Audio: a single sharp percussive hit synced to each disappearance — three hits, evenly spaced.
Ambient sound is muted and compressed between hits. No dialogue.
```

#### Shot 2 (7–13s) — escalation

```
Three more rapid slow-motion vignettes in the same glossy advertising style.
4. A single leather glove slips from a coat pocket onto a rainy pavement as a pair of legs walks
   on, out of focus behind it.
5. A small grey cat slips silently through a gap in a wooden garden gate at night and is gone
   into the dark.
6. A gold ring slides off a soapy finger and disappears down a kitchen sink drain, catching the
   light once on the way.
The cutting accelerates: each vignette is slightly shorter than the last.

Audio: percussive hits now closer together, tempo rising, a low tension tone building
underneath. No dialogue.
```

#### Shot 3 (13–18s) — the turn

```
All motion stops. A single slow, quiet, static shot: a plastic national identity card lies face
down on grey asphalt in the street, alone, slightly dusty. Blurred feet pass by around it, out
of focus, nobody stopping. The camera holds still. The lighting is flat and cold — every trace
of the glossy commercial look is gone. Then, very slowly, the camera pushes in one inch.

Audio: total silence for one beat, then only faint distant traffic. No music. No dialogue.
```

#### Post-production

- Overlay a running counter, one per vignette, in the corner: `1`, `2`, `3`, `4`, `5`, `6` — then
  on the final shot, nothing. The absence is the joke landing.
- Final overlay, held for the last two seconds: `Some of them, you really need back.` /
  `كاينة حوايج خاصك ترجع ليك.` / `Certaines, il faut vraiment les retrouver.` then the wordmark
  and `mafqoudat.com`.
- Music: a driving percussive track that cuts dead on the turn. The silence does the work.
- **Variation engine:** this exact template regenerates forever with different objects —
  a wallet, a school bag, a passport, a bicycle, a bunch of documents in the wind. Ship one a
  week.

**Caption (Arabic):** شنو ضاع ليك هاد العام؟ 👇 كتب ف الكومنتير.
**Caption (French):** Qu'est-ce que vous avez perdu cette année ? Le dernier, on ne rigole plus.
**Hashtags:** #ضاع_ليا #مفقودات #objetsperdus #lostandfound #Maroc

### Prompt 4 — "The Papers" (Pillar: High-stakes utility)

**The idea.** The one loss every Moroccan, Algerian and Tunisian viewer feels in their stomach:
losing your identity papers. A young man's ID wallet falls out of his jacket in a market. The
video follows the object, not the person — and ends with a stranger doing the right thing.

**Why it can travel.** Losing papers is not an inconvenience, it is weeks of queues and
paperwork. This video is saved and forwarded rather than merely liked, because it is
information people want their family to have.

**Hook (0.0–1.5s):** an ID wallet falling out of a jacket pocket in slow motion, in a crowded
market, with no one noticing.

**Target length:** 26 seconds (four shots).

#### Style block (repeat verbatim in every shot)

```
Cinematic live-action, handheld documentary energy, natural available light, rich warm colors
of a North African market, 35mm lens, shallow depth of field, slight film grain,
photorealistic. Tight framing that follows objects at waist height in a crowd. Vertical 9:16,
1080x1920, no text on screen, no readable writing on documents.
```

#### Shot 1 (0–7s)

```
A crowded outdoor market street in a Moroccan medina, mid-morning, stacked crates of oranges
and mint, striped awnings, dense foot traffic. Camera is low, at hip height, moving with the
crowd. A young man in a navy jacket pushes through the crowd, phone at his ear. A small brown
leather card-holder wallet works its way out of his jacket pocket in slow motion and drops
silently onto the dusty ground. He keeps walking and is swallowed by the crowd. The camera
stays with the wallet on the ground as dozens of feet pass around it.

Audio: dense market ambience — voices, a vendor calling out, a scooter engine, crates being
stacked. The moment the wallet lands, all sound drops away to near silence for one beat, then
returns. No dialogue.
```

#### Shot 2 (7–14s)

```
Same market street, same low camera height, same dusty ground. Feet keep passing the fallen
brown leather card-holder. One pair of worn sandals stops. A hand — an older woman's, with
henna on the fingers, a woven basket over the forearm — reaches down into frame and picks up
the wallet. The camera tilts up to her face: sun-lined, focused, kind, wearing a patterned
djellaba and a light headscarf. She opens the card-holder, looks at what is inside, then scans
the crowd around her, searching faces that are already gone.

Audio: market ambience returning at full volume, a vendor's call, birds. No dialogue.
```

#### Shot 3 (14–20s)

```
A quiet doorway just off the market street, shaded, cool blue light against the hot street
beyond. The same older woman in the patterned djellaba and light headscarf stands holding the
brown card-holder in one hand and an old smartphone in the other. She photographs the wallet
held flat against her palm, squinting at the screen, then types slowly and carefully with one
finger, lips moving slightly. Concentration, patience, a small public act of decency.

Audio: muffled market sound from around the corner, a camera shutter, soft footsteps. No
dialogue.
```

#### Shot 4 (20–26s)

```
A narrow residential street later the same day, long low sunlight. The young man in the navy
jacket hurries into frame from the left, out of breath. The older woman in the patterned
djellaba stands waiting by a doorway. She holds out the brown leather card-holder. He takes it
with both hands, presses it to his chest, and says something to her with his hand over his
heart. She waves it off, smiling, already turning to go. They part in opposite directions. The
camera holds on the empty street.

Audio: footsteps on stone, one short warm exchange of voices too quiet to make out, a distant
call to prayer. No clear dialogue.
```

#### Post-production

- Captions timed to the beats:
  - 1s — `Losing your papers costs weeks.` / `إلا ضاعو ليك الوراق… أسابيع ديال التعب.` / `Perdre ses papiers, c'est des semaines.`
  - 8s — `She didn't know him.` / `ماكانتش كتعرفو.` / `Elle ne le connaissait pas.`
  - 15s — `Two minutes. One photo. That's the whole thing.` / `دقيقتين. تصويرة وحدة. صافي.` / `Deux minutes. Une photo. C'est tout.`
  - 24s — `Mafqoudat — mafqoudat.com`
- Blur or avoid any legible detail on the document itself, at capture and again in the edit.
  A video about protecting people's papers must not show anyone's papers.
- Music: sparse, respectful strings entering only at shot 4.

**Caption (Arabic):** إلا لقيتي وراق ديال شي واحد، تقدر تعاونو ف دقيقتين. مجاناً، وبلا ما تعطي رقمك لأي حد.
**Caption (French):** Vous avez trouvé les papiers de quelqu'un ? Deux minutes suffisent pour les lui rendre.
**Hashtags:** #مفقودات #وراق #بطاقة_التعريف #objetsperdus #papiersperdus #Maroc

### Prompt 5 — "Is This Yours?" (Pillar: Community proof)

**The idea.** A street-documentary montage. Ordinary people in a Moroccan city hold up an
object to camera and ask a single question to no one in particular: *is this yours?* Different
ages, different neighbourhoods, different objects. It looks like a public service announcement
made by the public itself.

**Why it can travel.** It reads as real, not advertised — and it is directly copyable. The
format invites the audience to film their own version, which is exactly how a lost-and-found
network gets its supply side.

**Hook (0.0–1.5s):** a child holding a single small shoe up to the lens, dead centre, saying
nothing.

**Target length:** 20 seconds (three shots).

#### Style block (repeat verbatim in every shot)

```
Documentary street portraiture, shot on a handheld camera with natural light only, slight
imperfection and micro-shake, real skin texture, no beauty retouching, honest and unglamorous.
Subjects look directly into the lens, centred, waist-up, with their real neighbourhood visible
behind them in soft focus. Natural North African daylight. Vertical 9:16, 1080x1920, no text
on screen.
```

#### Shot 1 (0–7s)

```
Three consecutive street portraits, roughly two seconds each, all filmed the same way:
handheld, centred, subject looking directly into the lens on a real street.
1. A boy of about eight in a red football shirt on a residential street holds up a single small
   child's sandal toward the camera, deadpan, saying nothing.
2. A young woman in a denim jacket and white headscarf, standing in front of a blue-painted
   wall, holds up a set of car keys and gives them a small shake.
3. An elderly man in a beige djellaba on a café terrace holds up a pair of reading glasses,
   eyebrows raised in a silent question.
Each person holds their object steady toward the lens and waits, as if genuinely asking.

Audio: real ambient street sound for each location — children playing, a passing scooter, café
cups. No music yet. No dialogue.
```

#### Shot 2 (7–14s)

```
Three more street portraits in exactly the same handheld, centred, direct-to-lens style.
4. A young man in a mechanic's overalls, standing in a garage doorway, holds up a scuffed
   smartphone in a cracked case.
5. Two teenage girls share the frame on a school street; one holds up a small grey backpack,
   the other shrugs at the camera.
6. A woman in her forties in a market apron holds a small tabby cat carefully against her
   chest with both hands and looks straight into the lens, absolutely serious.
The cat portrait is held one beat longer than the others.

Audio: ambient sound of each location, a garage radio, schoolyard noise, market chatter. A warm
piano line begins under the last portrait. No dialogue.
```

#### Shot 3 (14–20s)

```
A slow montage of all six people from the previous shots, now each shown for less than a second
in the same framing, still holding their objects toward the lens — then the final frame: all of
them impossible together, standing in a loose group on one wide sunlit street, each still
holding their found object up toward the camera. Ordinary, unposed, warm late-afternoon light.
Nobody smiles for the camera; they just wait.

Audio: the warm piano line resolving, ambient street sound underneath. No dialogue.
```

#### Post-production

- One caption per portrait, in that person's voice: `Is this yours?` / `واش هادي ديالك؟` /
  `C'est à vous ?` — vary the phrasing slightly per person so it feels transcribed, not written.
- Final card: `Someone is looking for it right now.` / `كاين شي واحد كيقلب عليها دابا.` /
  `Quelqu'un la cherche en ce moment.` then the wordmark and `mafqoudat.com`.
- **This is the format to hand to the community.** Post a call to action: film yourself holding
  up what you found and ask the question. Repost the good ones. Supply-side growth for free.
- Music: single warm piano, no build, no drop.

**Caption (Arabic):** واش هادي ديالك؟ 👀 كل نهار كاين ناس كيلقاو حوايج ديال ناس خرين. صور اللي لقيتي وشاركو — يمكن يوصل لمولاه.
**Caption (French):** « C'est à vous ? » Chaque jour, quelqu'un trouve ce que quelqu'un d'autre a perdu.
**Hashtags:** #واش_هادي_ديالك #مفقودات #objetstrouvés #lostandfound #Maroc #Casablanca

## Part 3 — Running these as a campaign

**Order of release.** Start with prompt 3 ("Things I Have Lost This Year") for reach, follow
within 48 hours with prompt 2 ("Two Phones") so the audience that just laughed learns what the
thing actually does, then prompt 1 and 4 for emotional depth, and keep prompt 5 running
permanently as the community-supply engine.

**Cadence.** One new video every 2–3 days for the first month. Prompt 3's template alone can
carry two a week with different objects.

**What to measure.** Watch-through rate on the first 3 seconds tells you if the hook works;
shares and saves tell you if the story works; direct traffic to mafqoudat.com and new posts per
day tell you if any of it moved the product. Saves and shares matter more than likes here —
this is a utility people bookmark for a bad day.

**What never to do in these videos:**

- No invented statistics, no fake reunion counts, no testimonials from people who do not exist.
- No implication of in-app messaging, tracking, or an interactive map — the product has none of
  those.
- No legible personal data on any document, ever, in any frame.
- No promise of guaranteed recovery. The honest claim is that the platform *connects* the two
  sides — that is enough, and it is true.
